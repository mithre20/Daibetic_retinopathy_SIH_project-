# Retinal Image Capture Guidance for Rural Camps

## Frontline Capture Recommendations
For health workers and technicians using portable or desktop fundus cameras in rural clinics:

1. **Keep the Camera Steady**: Use both hands to support portable fundus devices or ensure the patient's chin and forehead rest firmly on the table-top slit-lamp/camera mount.
2. **Ensure Anatomical Field Visibility**: The optic nerve head (optic disc) and macula should be centered within the captured field. Major blood vessels should be visible branching outward.
3. **Avoid Excessive Glare & Reflections**: Glare occurs when the objective lens is too close or misaligned with the pupil axis. Back up slightly, realign the central beam, and advance smoothly until the reflection disappears.
4. **Avoid Motion Blur**: Instruct the patient to keep their eyes wide open and refrain from blinking or darting their eyes for 2 seconds while the camera captures the scan.
5. **Avoid Extreme Darkness & Underexposure**: Ensure sufficient flash intensity or sensor exposure. If pupil dilation is poor in ambient light, allow the patient 3 to 5 minutes in a darkened room for physiological dark adaptation.
6. **Keep the Eye Properly Positioned**: Use the internal fixation LED target so the patient's gaze remains stable.
7. **Follow Application Instructions**: If the IQA gate rejects the photo with a "Poor Quality" or "Unusable" notice, review the specific artifact flagged (e.g. blur, darkness, low contrast) and recapture immediately.
8. **Never Assume Quality**: Check the IQA status on Stage 1 before letting the patient leave the screening desk.
